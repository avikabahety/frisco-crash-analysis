Winter evening crashes at signalized intersections run measurably higher.

Winter evening crashes at Plano’s signalized intersections run measurably higher than summer. Left-turn crashes drive a disproportionate share of that difference.

Overall crash rates are nearly equal across seasons. The gap opens at 6pm–8pm — when winter is dark and summer is still light — and left-turn crashes account for a disproportionate share of it.

At 6pm–8pm, left-turn crashes run at 259 per 1,000 winter days versus 126 per 1,000 summer days — a rate ratio of 2.06× (95% CI 1.70–2.50). Those hours are dark in winter and light in summer.

comparisonThe pattern is most prominent when light conditions differ

Sunset in Plano falls around 5:25pm in December and 8:35pm in June; sunrise around 7:25am in December and 6:15am in June. The chart covers 6am to 11pm. Winter is dark while summer is light in two windows: in the evening from 6pm to 8pm, and briefly in the early morning before 7am. The evening window is the one this analysis tests; the morning window is shorter and has too few crashes for a reliable comparison.

At midday, when both seasons are light, winter and summer left-turn crash rates track closely (0.93×). After 9pm, when both are dark, winter runs lower than summer (0.62×). A seasonal factor that raises winter evening crashes, such as school-term traffic or holiday driving, would be expected to show up in those hours too; it does not.

robustnessChecks performed

Each of the following would be expected to remove the difference if the stated alternative explanation held. The observed difference persists in each case.

  • —
    Dry pavement only. Winter is both darker and wetter. Restricting to crashes where the officer recorded a dry road surface (from the CRIS surface condition field) does not reduce the difference, so wet roads do not explain it. all surfaces: +7.6 pp · dry pavement only: +8.4 pp
  • —
    Other crash types. If darkness simply made all driving harder, every type of crash would rise by a similar amount in the 6pm–8pm window. It does not: left-turn crashes roughly double, right-angle crashes do not rise, and rear-end crashes rise only modestly. left-turn 2.06× · right-angle 0.74× · rear-end 1.26× · right-turn 0.86× (few crashes)

The pattern also holds within individual intersections: among the 113 intersections with enough crashes to compare, left turns make up a larger part of crashes after dark than in daylight at 65. It also holds after excluding impaired-driver crashes (+8.0 pp, impairment recorded in 2.9% of crashes in these hours).

limitationsWhat this analysis does not establish

These bound the interpretation of everything above.

  • Exposure is unknown. The number of left turns attempted at these hours in each season is not available. If winter evenings carry less traffic than summer evenings, the risk per turn is higher than these figures indicate; if winter evenings carry more traffic than summer evenings, then the risk is lower.
  • Reportable crashes only. CRIS contains crashes for which an officer filed a CR-3: injury, death, or $1,000+ in property damage. Minor crashes and those without police response are not included.
  • Whether the increase concentrates at specific intersection types is not confirmed. The analysis attempted to compare intersections that appear more permissive against those that appear less so; the two groups are not statistically distinguishable (p = 0.45). Intersections with protected-only phasing produce few left-turn crashes and rarely meet the minimum count needed to be classified, so the comparison is inherently limited.

methodHow the data was cut

All crash records held by TxDOT for Plano, 2016–2025, narrowed to those that can be placed at a named intersection at a known time.

RecordsStep
55,415CRIS export
24,020at or related to an intersection (signalized, stop-controlled, and other)
22,237both street names present
22,237time and date parse
22,237on or before 2025-12-31
12,407at signalized intersections — analysis population

Crashes the officer coded as not related to an intersection, largely freeway and mid-block crashes, are excluded, as are driveway-access crashes. The analyses above use the signalized subset, since left-turn phasing exists only at a signal.
