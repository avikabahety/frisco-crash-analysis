Winter evening crashes at Frisco’s signalised intersections run measurably higher than summer. Left-turn crashes drive a disproportionate share of that difference.

the numbersZooming in on the 6–8pm window

Overall crash rates are nearly equal across seasons. The gap opens at 6–8pm — when winter is dark and summer is still light — and left-turn crashes account for a disproportionate share of it.

At 6–8pm, left-turn crashes run at 134 per 1,000 winter days versus 64 per 1,000 summer days — a rate ratio of 2.09× (95% CI 1.59–2.73). Those hours are dark in winter and light in summer.

comparisonThe pattern is most prominent when light conditions differ

Sunset in Frisco falls around 5:25pm in December and 8:35pm in June; sunrise around 7:25am in December and 6:15am in June. The chart covers 6am to 10pm, with two windows where winter is dark and summer is light: around 7am and from 6–8pm.

Outside those windows winter and summer left-turn crashes track closely — at midday both seasons are light, after 9pm both are dark. A seasonal factor unrelated to light — school-term traffic, holiday driving, winter road conditions — would be expected to affect those hours too. It does not.

robustnessChecks performed

Each of the following would be expected to remove the difference if the stated alternative explanation held. The observed difference persists in each case.

  • —
    Dry pavement only. Winter is both darker and wetter. Restricting to crashes where the officer recorded dry road surface (from the CRIS surface condition field) leaves the difference unchanged, ruling out road conditions as an explanation. dry pavement only: +9.6 pp, p < 0.001
  • —
    Right-turn comparison. If night driving were generally more difficult, other turning movements would shift similarly. Right-turn crashes show no change in the same window, making a general night-driving explanation unlikely. left-turn +9.6 pp · right-turn -0.9 pp · right-angle -5.4 pp
  • —
    Rates, not counts. Left-turn crashes are compared as rates per calendar day rather than as shares of total crashes, so a shrinking denominator cannot produce a spurious rise. 6pm: 1.96× left-turn vs 1.46× all crashes

The association also survives stratification by intersection (Mantel–Haenszel OR 1.50 across 39 intersections, 25/39 moving the predicted way) and exclusion of impaired-driver crashes (+10.9 pp, impairment recorded in 3.9% of crashes in these hours).

limitationsWhat this analysis does not establish

These bound the interpretation of everything above.

  • Exposure is unknown. The number of left turns attempted after dark is not available. Traffic volumes are lower at night, so the risk per turn attempted is likely higher than these figures indicate, making the estimates conservative.
  • Reportable crashes only. CRIS contains crashes for which an officer filed a CR-3: injury, death, or $1,000+ in property damage. Minor crashes and those without police response are not included.
  • Whether the increase concentrates at specific intersection types is not confirmed. The analysis attempted to compare intersections that appear more permissive against those that appear less so; the two groups are not statistically distinguishable (p = 0.52). Intersections with protected-only phasing produce few left-turn crashes and rarely meet the minimum count needed to be classified, so the comparison is inherently limited.

methodHow the data was cut

All crash records held by TxDOT for Frisco, 2016–2025, narrowed to those that can be placed at a named intersection at a known time.

RecordsStep
28,342CRIS export
13,358at or related to an intersection (signalised, stop-controlled, and other)
12,847both street names present
12,847time and date parse
12,195on or before 2025-12-31
7,022at signalised intersections — analysis population

Freeway mainlane crashes are excluded: none carry a cross street and their crash-type distribution is characteristic of a freeway, not an intersection. Driveway-access crashes are excluded. The analyses above use the signalised subset, since left-turn phasing exists only at a signal.
